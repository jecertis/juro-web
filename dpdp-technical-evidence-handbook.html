DPDP §8

The DPDP Technical Evidence Handbook: Proving the Security Safeguards under Section 8

JuroCompliant · 17 July 2026 · ~18 min read
Who this is for: CTOs, security engineers, and DPOs who need to evidence the technical half of DPDP §8 with cloud-configuration facts, not a policy PDF — plus DPO consultancies and CA firms who own the legal half.

For every technical security safeguard the DPDP Act asks a data fiduciary to take, this handbook shows the concrete cloud-configuration fact that evidences it and the exact scanner check that verifies it. It covers the engineering half of DPDP. The legal half (consent, notice, data-principal rights) belongs to your law firm.

Note on scope, per Axiom 2

This handbook evidences and demonstrates the technical safeguards under §8. It does not make you "DPDP compliant" and the scanner does not certify compliance. It proves that specific controls are in place. Compliance is a legal determination made by a person, not a scan. Every control below is written to be read on its own. If you quote a single answer, it should stay true and stay honest without the rest of the page.

A word on why the technical half matters. DPDP §8(5) requires a data fiduciary to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach. The penalty schedule attaches up to ₹250 crore to a failure of that duty, the highest tier in the Act. That is not a documentation exercise. It is a question about the actual state of the systems that hold personal data. This handbook is about proving that state with facts, not asserting it with a policy PDF.

Each entry below is a question a real person types, followed by five short lines: the DPDP clause, what the law asks in plain English, what technically proves it, the Juro rule id or ids that verify it with their cited legal basis, and where to scan your own environment.

1. Access Control and Identity

The most common way personal data leaks is not a broken encryption algorithm. It is an identity that could reach the data and should not have been able to. DPDP §8(5) requires reasonable security safeguards, and Schedule 1 to the Act names access control and authentication among them. Access is the first safeguard any auditor will ask you to evidence.

Are root access keys a DPDP problem?

DPDP clause
§8(5) reasonable security safeguards.
What the law asks
§8(5) requires reasonable safeguards on systems that process personal data. A cloud root or owner identity bypasses every permission boundary you set up, so a leaked root key is a full compromise of everything, including the data.
What technically proves it
The account has no active root access keys. Programmatic access runs through scoped, least-privilege roles, never root.
Juro rule
AWS-IAM-ROOT-001 flags an IAM root account that still has active access keys. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate security safeguards for personal data processing systems.

Does DPDP require MFA?

DPDP clause
Schedule 1 §4 authentication safeguards, supporting §8(5).
What the law asks
Schedule 1 to the Act lists multi-factor authentication among the safeguards expected for systems processing personal data. A password alone is not a reasonable safeguard for access to personal data in 2026.
What technically proves it
The identity provider or user pool fronting your application requires a second factor, not just a password.
Juro rule
AWS-COG-MFA-001 flags a Cognito user pool that does not require MFA. Its cited legal basis is DPDP Schedule 1 §4, multi-factor authentication for systems processing personal data.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Does DPDP require you to rotate access keys?

DPDP clause
Schedule 1 §3 credential lifecycle management, supporting §8(5).
What the law asks
A reasonable safeguard is not a one-time setup. Schedule 1 expects credential lifecycle management. A service-account key that has been valid and unrotated for a year is a standing risk, because you no longer know every place it has been copied.
What technically proves it
Long-lived, user-managed keys are rotated on a bounded schedule. No active key is older than your rotation window.
Juro rule
GCP-IAM-KEY-001 flags a service account with user-managed keys older than 90 days. Its cited legal basis is DPDP Schedule 1 §3, credential lifecycle management.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Is a public Kubernetes cluster endpoint a DPDP concern?

DPDP clause
Schedule 1 §3 access controls, supporting §8(5).
What the law asks
Schedule 1 §3 expects access controls that restrict who can reach the systems that process personal data. A Kubernetes control plane exposed to the whole internet is reachable by anyone, which is the opposite of a restricted surface.
What technically proves it
The cluster API endpoint is private, or its public access is restricted to known networks rather than open to the world.
Juro rule
AWS-EKS-PUB-001 flags an EKS cluster whose API server endpoint is publicly accessible. Its cited legal basis is DPDP Schedule 1 §3, access controls restricting exposure of systems processing personal data.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Can a publicly readable storage bucket count against DPDP?

DPDP clause
Schedule 1 §3 access controls, supporting §8(5).
What the law asks
Access to personal data must be restricted to identities that need it. A storage bucket that grants read access to all users, or to anyone with a link, has no access control at all on the data inside it.
What technically proves it
Bucket-level access is not granted to allUsers or allAuthenticatedUsers. Access resolves to named principals only.
Juro rule
GCP-STORAGE-ACCESS-001 flags a Cloud Storage bucket that grants access to all users or all authenticated users. Its cited legal basis is DPDP Schedule 1 §3, access controls for personal data.

2. Network Exposure and Public Access

Access control decides who may reach an identity. Network control decides what is reachable in the first place. DPDP §8(5) and Schedule 1 §3 both point at keeping the systems that process personal data off the open internet unless there is a reason for them to be on it.

Should databases holding personal data sit in a private subnet?

DPDP clause
§8(5) reasonable security safeguards.
What the law asks
§8(5) expects reasonable technical safeguards. Network segmentation is one of the oldest and most basic. A data store that lives in a public subnet is one misconfigured rule away from being reachable from anywhere.
What technically proves it
Workloads that process or store personal data run in private subnets with no direct route to or from the public internet.
Juro rule
AWS-VPC-PRI-001 flags data-processing workloads placed in public subnets. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate technical safeguards, where network segmentation is a baseline security control for personal data processing infrastructure.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Is unrestricted outbound network access a DPDP risk?

DPDP clause
§8(5) reasonable security safeguards.
What the law asks
A safeguard is not only about who gets in. It is also about where data can go. A workload that can send traffic to any destination on the internet has no boundary on where personal data can be copied to if it is compromised.
What technically proves it
Security groups on personal-data workloads restrict egress to known destinations rather than allowing all outbound traffic.
Juro rule
AWS-SG-EGR-001 flags a security group that permits unrestricted outbound access. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate technical safeguards, where unrestricted egress from personal data processing workloads undermines data-boundary controls.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

What does DPDP expect from firewall rules?

DPDP clause
Schedule 1 §3 safeguards against unauthorised access, supporting §8(5).
What the law asks
Schedule 1 §3 expects reasonable safeguards against unauthorised access. A firewall rule that opens a port to the entire internet is an invitation, not a safeguard.
What technically proves it
Firewall rules do not allow ingress from 0.0.0.0/0 to sensitive ports. Access is scoped to known source ranges.
Juro rule
GCP-VPC-FW-002 flags overly permissive firewall rules that allow unrestricted ingress. Its cited legal basis is DPDP Schedule 1 §3, reasonable security safeguards against unauthorised access.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Should Kubernetes nodes have public IP addresses?

DPDP clause
Schedule 1 §3 network isolation, supporting §8(5).
What the law asks
Schedule 1 §3 expects network-level isolation for systems that handle personal data. Cluster nodes with public IP addresses expand the reachable surface of workloads that may be processing personal data.
What technically proves it
The cluster uses private nodes with no public IP addresses. Node-to-internet traffic, where needed, routes through controlled egress.
Juro rule
GCP-GKE-NET-001 flags a GKE cluster whose nodes are not private. Its cited legal basis is DPDP Schedule 1 §3, network-level isolation for systems handling personal data.

3. Encryption in Transit and at Rest

Encryption is where reasonable safeguards become concrete. DPDP §8(5) and Schedule 1 §3 expect personal data to be protected both while it moves across a network and while it sits on a disk. A short, honest note on tagging is at the end of this section, because it matters for how you read the scan output.

Does DPDP require HTTPS or TLS for data in transit?

DPDP clause
§8(5) safeguards for personal data in transit.
What the law asks
Personal data moving between a user and your service, or between your own services, should not travel as plaintext. §8(5) treats encryption in transit as a baseline technical safeguard.
What technically proves it
The content delivery or edge layer enforces a modern TLS policy and does not accept plaintext or deprecated protocol versions.
Juro rule
AWS-CF-TLS-001 flags a CloudFront distribution that permits weak or outdated TLS. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate technical safeguards for personal data in transit, and GDPR Art. 32 for encryption and confidentiality of personal data.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Does a managed database need encrypted connections under DPDP?

DPDP clause
Schedule 1 §3 encryption standards, supporting §8(5).
What the law asks
A managed database that accepts unencrypted connections lets personal data cross the network in the clear between the application and the database. Schedule 1 §3 expects encryption standards to apply to that path.
What technically proves it
The database instance requires SSL or TLS for all client connections and rejects unencrypted ones.
Juro rule
GCP-SQL-ENC-001 flags a Cloud SQL instance that does not enforce encryption standards for connections. Its cited legal basis is DPDP Schedule 1 §3, encryption standards for personal data.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Do secrets and parameters need customer-controlled encryption keys?

DPDP clause
§8(5) reasonable security safeguards.
What the law asks
The secrets that unlock personal data, such as database passwords and API keys, are themselves sensitive. §8(5) treats their protection as part of the safeguard. Customer-controlled keys mean you, not only the platform, govern who can decrypt.
What technically proves it
Secure parameters and secrets are encrypted with a customer-managed key rather than only a default platform key.
Juro rule
AWS-SSM-CMK-001 flags SSM parameters that are not encrypted with a customer-managed key. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate security safeguards, specifically customer-controlled encryption keys for personal data parameters.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Is personal data encrypted at rest, and how does DPDP read that check?

DPDP clause
§8(5) reasonable security safeguards, evidenced through encryption at rest.
What the law asks
Personal data sitting on a disk in object storage, in a database, or in a warehouse should be encrypted at rest so that raw storage access does not expose it. Encryption at rest is a core §8(5) safeguard.
What technically proves it
Buckets, databases, warehouses, and the keys that protect them all enforce encryption at rest, and key access policy is scoped rather than open.
Juro rules
AWS-S3-ENC-001 (object storage), AWS-RDS-ENC-001 (relational databases), AWS-KMS-POL-001 (key access policy), GCP-STORAGE-ENC-001 (object storage), and GCP-BQ-ENC-001 (data warehouse) each verify encryption at rest for their respective store.
An honest note on tagging

In the scanner today these five checks carry a GDPR Art. 32 legal basis, encryption of personal data, which is the same safeguard the DPDP Act asks for. The scan output for these five does not yet print a DPDP tag. The at-rest encryption control they verify is the same technical fact that evidences DPDP §8(5), and a DPDP citation crosswalk is the source of truth that maps them across. Read them as evidence of the safeguard. The DPDP tag can be added to the scan output when a customer or partner needs the report itself to carry it. Do not read the current scan output as showing a DPDP citation for these five.

4. Logging and Audit Trails

You cannot prove a safeguard held if you kept no record of who touched the data. DPDP §8(5) carries an accountability dimension: a data fiduciary should be able to reconstruct access to personal data stores. Logging is how that becomes evidence rather than assertion.

Does DPDP require logging access to personal data stores?

DPDP clause
§8(5) accountability and record-keeping.
What the law asks
An audit trail of who read or changed personal data is what lets you answer, after the fact, whether an access was legitimate. §8(5) treats that trail as a technical accountability measure.
What technically proves it
Data-plane event logging is enabled on the stores that hold personal data, capturing object-level and record-level access.
Juro rule
AWS-CT-DATA-001 flags missing CloudTrail data-event logging on personal data stores. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate security safeguards, where an audit trail of access to personal data stores is a technical accountability measure.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Should you have automated alerting on your logs?

DPDP clause
§8(5) reasonable security safeguards.
What the law asks
A log nobody watches is a record for the post-mortem, not a safeguard in the moment. §8(5) reasonable safeguards include noticing a problem while it is happening. Automated metric filters and alerts turn passive logs into active detection.
What technically proves it
Metric filters and alarms are configured on the log groups that matter, so anomalous activity raises an alert rather than sitting silently.
Juro rule
AWS-CWL-MF-001 flags log groups without the expected metric filters and alerting. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate technical safeguards, specifically automated monitoring and alerting for personal data processing systems.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Does DPDP expect audit logs to be centralised and retained?

DPDP clause
§8(5) accountability and record-keeping.
What the law asks
Audit records scattered across projects, or discarded quickly, cannot support accountability. §8(5) record-keeping expects logs to be routed to durable storage where they survive and can be reviewed.
What technically proves it
An audit log sink exports logs to durable, centralised storage rather than leaving them to expire in place.
Juro rule
GCP-AUDIT-SINK-001 flags a missing or misconfigured audit log sink. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), accountability and record-keeping for personal data processing.

5. Retention and Deletion

Holding personal data longer than you need it is its own risk. DPDP §8(3) sets a retention limitation, and §8(9) requires that personal data not be kept beyond the period necessary. Automated lifecycle and expiry rules are how you evidence that a limit exists and actually fires.

Does DPDP require you to delete data you no longer need?

DPDP clause
§8(9) retention limitation, safeguarded under §8(5).
What the law asks
§8(9) says personal data must not be retained beyond the period necessary. A bucket that keeps every object forever has no retention limit at all. An automated lifecycle policy is the technical safeguard that enforces the limit.
What technically proves it
Object storage that holds personal data carries a lifecycle policy that expires or transitions objects on a defined schedule.
Juro rule
AWS-S3-LCP-001 flags an S3 bucket without a lifecycle policy. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(9), data fiduciaries must not retain personal data beyond the period necessary, where an automated lifecycle policy is a safeguard under §8(5).
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Should warehouse tables expire personal data automatically?

DPDP clause
§8(3) retention limitation.
What the law asks
A data warehouse is where personal data quietly accumulates. §8(3) retention limitation expects a bound on how long it stays. Table and partition expiry is the technical form of that bound.
What technically proves it
Warehouse datasets and tables holding personal data have a default expiry configured, so old rows age out rather than piling up indefinitely.
Juro rule
GCP-BQ-EXPIRY-001 flags BigQuery datasets or tables with no expiry configured. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(3), data retention limitation for personal data.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Does DPDP expect a defined retention setting on storage?

DPDP clause
§8(3) retention limitation.
What the law asks
Retention limitation works both ways. You need data to age out when it is no longer needed, and you need the retention behaviour to be a deliberate setting rather than an accident. §8(3) expects that behaviour to be defined.
What technically proves it
Storage buckets holding personal data have a retention configuration that reflects a deliberate policy rather than an unbounded default.
Juro rule
GCP-STORAGE-RETAIN-001 flags storage buckets without a defined retention configuration. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(3), data retention limitation.

6. Backup, Resilience and Breach Detection

This section is thin, and we will say so plainly. The scanner has early coverage here, not a full resilience posture. What follows is honest about being a starting point. DPDP §8(7) speaks to the accuracy and recoverability of personal data, and §8(5) to detecting unauthorised access. The checks below evidence pieces of that, not the whole of it.

Does DPDP require backups of personal data?

DPDP clause
§8(7) recoverability, safeguarded under §8(5).
What the law asks
§8(7) speaks to keeping personal data accurate and recoverable. If a store is lost or corrupted and there is no backup, recoverability fails. Backup readiness is the technical safeguard behind it.
What technically proves it
Critical data stores are covered by an active backup plan rather than left with no recovery path.
Juro rule
AWS-BKP-EMP-001 flags an empty or absent backup plan on resources that should be protected. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(7), data fiduciaries must ensure accuracy and recoverability of personal data, where backup readiness is a security safeguard under §8(5).
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Is object versioning relevant to DPDP?

DPDP clause
§8(7) completeness and accuracy.
What the law asks
§8(7) expects personal data to stay complete and accurate. Versioning protects against an accidental or malicious overwrite silently destroying the correct version of a record, which is a recoverability safeguard.
What technically proves it
Buckets holding personal data have versioning enabled, so an overwrite or delete leaves a recoverable prior version.
Juro rule
AWS-S3-VER-001 flags an S3 bucket without versioning enabled. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(7), data fiduciaries must ensure completeness and accuracy of personal data.
Scan your own environment for this at jurocompliant.com.

Does DPDP expect threat detection on data stores?

DPDP clause
§8(5) detection of unauthorised access.
What the law asks
§8(5) reasonable safeguards include noticing unauthorised access to personal data stores. Threat detection tuned to those stores is how suspicious access becomes an alert rather than an unnoticed event.
What technically proves it
Managed threat detection is enabled and configured to monitor the storage that holds personal data.
Juro rule
AWS-GD-S3-001 flags GuardDuty not being configured to monitor S3 data stores. Its cited legal basis is DPDP §8(5), appropriate technical safeguards including detection of unauthorised access to personal data stores.

7. Advisory: outside the scanner

The two subsections below are not scanner output. They are the two honest gaps where DPDP asks for something the scanner does not, and should not, try to detect. We name them rather than skip them, because a named gap routed to the right partner is more useful than a silent omission. Treat everything in Section 7 as advisory. Route it to your legal or CA partner.

Advisory — not scanner output

7a. Processor and Vendor (DPA) obligations

DPDP clause
Processor and data-processing-agreement obligations between a data fiduciary and the data processors it engages.
What the law asks
Where you engage a processor to handle personal data on your behalf, DPDP expects that relationship to be governed by contract, with the processor bound to appropriate obligations. This is a contract and governance question.
What proves it
Signed data-processing agreements, a maintained vendor register, and contractual controls flowed down to each processor. None of that is a cloud-configuration fact, so the scanner has zero technical coverage here by design.
Where this goes
Your legal or CA partner. This is exactly the hand-off surface a DPO consultancy or CA firm is built to own.
Advisory — not scanner output

7b. Breach notification to the Board and data principals (§8(6))

DPDP clause
§8(6), the duty to notify the Data Protection Board and affected data principals of a personal data breach.
What the law asks
On a breach, §8(6) requires notification to the Board and to affected data principals within the statutory process and timelines. The penalty schedule attaches up to ₹200 crore to a failure of this duty, so it is not a formality.
What proves it
A documented breach-response runbook, defined notification timelines, and evidence that the process has been exercised. The scanner can help you detect a breach, and Section 6 covers pieces of that detection, but the duty to notify is a legal and operational process, not a configuration state.
Where this goes
Your legal or CA partner, paired with your incident-response process.
